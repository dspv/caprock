# The personal channel

Status: **concept agreed, nothing published yet.** The name is still open.

## What it is

A personal channel by an engineer who builds his own things at night and
**measures everything**. Caprock appears in it as "here is what I built and what
it measured" — alongside experiments, a product that was closed, and the reality
of full-time work plus a side project.

The value of the channel is the person, not the product. Products change; the
channel is the asset that survives them and can launch the next one.

## Position

**Doesn't take claims on trust — attaches a counter.**

Opinions about AI agents are infinite; measured data is nearly absent. The owner
has 35 days, $10,063 and 70,199 tool calls of his own usage. That is not an
opinion, so there is nothing to argue with.

The second layer is what makes it personal: he has **already closed a product**,
honestly and with a written postmortem. Most indie channels show only the climbs.

## Topics

Four, all his.

**1. AI agents in practice.** How working with Claude Code, OpenCode and the rest
actually goes: measurements, traps, techniques. The home territory — this is
where he holds data nobody else has.

**2. Building products.** Idea to shutdown. What gets built, what gets abandoned,
and why. Money and postmortems included.

**3. Engineering measurement generally.** "What X actually costs." Not only AI:
infrastructure, speed, the price of a decision.

**4. The indie-maker life.** Full-time job plus one's own work at night: time,
choices, burnout. The most personal layer.

The first three earn usefulness; the fourth earns attachment. Without the fourth
the channel is a reference manual; without the first three it is a diary.

## What is not in it

- Retellings of other people's news and releases
- "10 prompts that will change your life"
- Unverified claims. Not measured means said to be not measured
- Caprock in every post. It is an instrument inside a story, not the subject

## Rhythm

- **One substantial post a week** — the core
- **One or two short ones** between: an observation, a screenshot, a question
- Video only when a post landed. Never on a schedule

Sized for one to two hours a week, which is what the owner actually has.
Promising more produces guilt, not posts.

## Two platforms

| Platform | Language | What goes there                                             |
| -------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Telegram | Russian  | Written the way he thinks: longer, reflective, with feeling |
| X        | English  | The same finding: number in the first line, plus an image   |

**Repackaging, not translation.** A Telegram post opens with the story; an X post
opens with the figure. One finding, two entrances. Drafts are prepared for him;
he edits them into his own voice.

The Russian-speaking audience is not expected to convert into installs directly.
It is where he writes fastest and most naturally, and where recommendations,
stars and word-of-mouth come from. The English repackaging is what reaches people
who run `brew install`.

## The name

Decided form: **first name plus topic, no surname.**

Russian (Telegram): "Дима меряет", "Ночная смена", "Дима строит и меряет".
English (X): `@dima_builds`, `@dimameasures`, `@nightshift_dev`.

Handles have to be checked by hand at registration — X returns 404 for private
accounts too, so an automated check proves nothing.

## Topic bank

One per rubric, all from real data.

1. **$10,063 in 35 days.** What development with an agent actually costs once
   measured rather than estimated *(agents)*
2. **The average session is 39 hours.** Not the 20 minutes every demo shows, and
   what that changes about how we work *(agents)*
3. **Half of all tool calls are bash.** The agent runs commands more than it
   writes code — data against intuition *(measurement)*
4. **Closing a product after months of work.** Compression delivered 0%,
   attribution turned out to be native in AWS, and why quitting was correct
   *(products)*
5. **Full-time plus a side project.** How many hours are actually left and what
   fits in them *(indie life)*

## Success criteria

Three numbers; everything else is vanity.

- **Telegram subscribers** — is the asset growing
- **Clicks to caprock.dev from X** — does the repackaging work
- **`copy-command` in Umami** — does it reach an install

If after six weeks the first grows and the third does not, the channel is useful
as an asset but not as a funnel. That is an acceptable outcome, provided it is
named honestly rather than explained away.
