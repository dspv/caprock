# Channel research

What was actually checked, with dates. Negative findings carry the same weight as
positive ones — a channel ruled out on evidence saves more time than one that was
never examined.

## Telegram Ads

**Checked: 2026-08-24. Status: viable, deliberately postponed.**

The owner bought a course on Telegram Ads. Its appeal is real: the platform sells
traffic in amounts small enough to experiment with (€20–60), and it buys the one
thing a person with one to two hours a week cannot produce — reach.

Two corrections to the course's framing:

- **A click is not a subscriber.** Advertised clicks run €1–3, but 20–40% of
  arrivals subscribe, which puts a subscriber at **€3–10**. A €60 test therefore
  buys 6–20 subscribers — too few to distinguish a working creative from a bad
  one.
- **A minimum honest test is €150–200.** Below that the result is noise, and
  noise read as signal is worse than no test.

**Postponed until the channel has 15–20 posts.** Advertising into an empty
channel converts poorly: the visitor arrives, finds nothing to read, and leaves.
The course does not expire.

## Directories and listings

**Checked: 2026-08-23. Status: mostly not worth it.**

Submitting to AI-tool directories was investigated and largely ruled out. The
largest one measured carries roughly 2,100 visitors a month **in total across the
whole site**, so a single listing's share is negligible. Product Hunt no longer
produces the traffic it did.

**The exception is `awesome-claude-code`** — a curated list where the audience is
exactly right. It has a 14-day rule (a project must be published that long before
submission, which is satisfied after 2026-09-01) and accepts submissions through
an **issue form, not a pull request**.

## Findings-led posts

**Checked: 2026-08-23. Status: the strongest organic format.**

Comparing posts in the relevant communities: a post that reports a measurement
scored 112 points, while posts announcing a tool scored 1–3. The pattern is
consistent enough to plan around — lead with what was found, not with what was
built.

This is the reasoning behind the channel's "Measurement" rubric being the core
rather than one rubric among four.

## Reddit

**Checked: 2026-08-24. Status: downgraded, not ruled out.**

Google reduced Reddit's search visibility, which removes the long-tail value that
made a Reddit post worth writing months later. Still viable for direct reach into
a subreddit, but no longer worth optimising for search.

## Telegram organic

**Observed: 2026-08-24. Status: works, sample of one.**

The owner showed Caprock in a small chat; the chat's owner reposted it to a large
one. Result over the following hours: **82 visitors, 14 of whom copied the
install command — a 17% install-intent rate.** Traffic came without a referrer
(Telegram strips it), so it appears as direct in analytics.

The geography was much wider than the source chat: United States, Netherlands,
Germany, Cyprus, Spain, Georgia, Poland, Sweden, France, with Russia only 4 of
54. 28% of visits came from iOS, where the product cannot actually be installed.

One data point, not a trend. But it is evidence that a single well-placed post
outperforms a directory listing by an order of magnitude.
