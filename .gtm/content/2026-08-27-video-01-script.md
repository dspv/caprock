# Video 1 — "Half of it is running commands"

**Length:** 60–90 seconds
**Shows:** the dashboard, screen only — no face, no surname
**Where:** YouTube (as a Short), X, LinkedIn
**Status:** ready to record — figures re-read 2026-08-28

---

## What this video is

One finding, said once, with the screen behind it. Not a tour, not a feature
list, not an install guide. A person who wants a tour is already convinced;
this is aimed at the one who is not.

The finding: **half of what Claude Code costs is running commands, not writing
code.** It works because everybody assumes the opposite, and because it is
checkable — the viewer can install the thing and get their own version of it
in a minute.

## The script

Timings are guides, not marks. Read it at the pace of talking, not presenting.

**0:00–0:08 — the number, cold open**

> On screen: the Now page, ALL TIME strip filling the top.

"Three months of Claude Code. Eleven thousand seven hundred of usage at
list prices. I paid six hundred and forty-seven."

**0:08–0:20 — the turn**

> On screen: scroll to the Cost page, "What it went on".

"But that is not the interesting part. This is."

*(pause — let the breakdown land before saying anything about it)*

**0:20–0:38 — the finding**

> On screen: the "what it went on" rows, cursor resting on the top one.

"Half of it — forty-eight percent — went on running commands. Tests, builds,
`git status` for the hundredth time. Writing code was thirteen."

"I had this backwards. I assumed the expensive part was the model writing
things. It is not. The expensive part is running what it wrote."

**0:38–0:52 — why it matters to the viewer**

> On screen: back to Now, live activity moving.

"And I would never have known. The plan is flat — two hundred a month, nothing
itemises. You cannot see any of this from the invoice, because there isn't one."

**0:52–1:05 — what it is**

> On screen: the terminal or the projects panel, whatever is moving.

"So this reads the transcripts Claude Code already writes to disk and prices
every turn. It runs on my machine. Nothing leaves it, there is no account, and
it is open source."

**1:05–1:15 — the ask**

> On screen: the install command, or the share card.

"Your history is already on your disk. This just reads it. Caprock dot dev."

## The last line, and why it is that one

Not "download Caprock". Not "link in bio". **"Your history is already on your
disk"** is the only sentence here that is about the viewer rather than about
me, and it is true in a way that surprises people: the figures are there on
first run, for every session they have ever run, not from installation onward.

## What not to say

- Never "saves you money". It does not, and one comment pointing that out
  undoes the whole video.
- No multiple — "18×" invites an argument about the denominator. The two
  figures side by side make the same point and cannot be argued with.
- No feature list. One finding. Everything else is the next video.
- Nothing that `caprock report` does not print.
